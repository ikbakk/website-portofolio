From the interface people use to the services and data that keep it dependable.

The interface is only one part of the feature. I follow the decisions behind it, through the service, the data, and the release.

A feature crosses layers.

The seams matter. A small decision in one layer can shape the behavior of the whole product.

I keep the user's path and the system's behavior in the same conversation.

  • Interface

    States, edge cases, accessibility, and feedback belong in the feature from the start.

  • Services

    Clear boundaries make behavior easier to understand, change, and recover when something fails.

  • Data

    Model the lifecycle as well as the shape. What changes, what persists, and what can be safely retried?

  • Delivery

    A feature is not finished until it can be observed, deployed, and improved with confidence.

Make the trade-offs visible.

Good engineering is not about adding layers for their own sake. It is about making the important choices explicit: where validation belongs, what can fail, and how a future change will move through the system.

That keeps the work understandable for the people building it and predictable for the people relying on it.
